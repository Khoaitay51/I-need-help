1 In DNA, the sugar is 2'-deoxyribose. 2  In RNA, the sugar is ribose. 3  Both ribose and deoxyribose are 5-csrbon sugars. 4  The carbons are numbered sequentially, to help keep track of where groups are attached. 5  The only difference between them is that 2'-deoxyribose has one less oxygen atom attached to the second carbon. When nucleotides connect to form DNA or RNA, the phosphate of one nucleotide attaches via a phosphodiester bond to the 3-carbon of the sugar of the next nucleotide, forming the sugar-phosphate backbone of the nucleic acid.
