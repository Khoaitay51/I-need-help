Synaptic signals from other neurons are received by the soma and dendrites; signals to other neurons are transmitted by the axon. A typical synapse, then, is a contact between the axon of one neuron and a dendrite or soma of another. Synaptic signals may be excitatory or inhibitory. There are also other types of neurons that have consistent effects on their targets, for example excitatory motor neurons in the spinal cord that release acetylcholine, and inhibitory spinal neurons that release glycine. The distinction between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitters is not absolute, however.
