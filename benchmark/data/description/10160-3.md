Peer Review Status: Internally Peer Reviewed Absence of the levator palpebrae superioris has been noted. When the muscle is present, a slip (tensor trochleae [Budge]) may arise from its belly or proximal part and may pass to the sclera, trochlea of the superior oblique, or neighboring tissues. 1 Even Vesalius reported that the levator may be doubled. 2  According to Macalister, Boldrini (1669) saw a similar slip but called it a fifth rectus muscle as did Kulmus (1724). 3  Macalister described a case of absence of levator palpebra superioris existing without ptosis.
