Jack Tormoehlen Cells use ATP for energy. The energy comes from releasing a phosphate group from ATP to create ADP. This energy is used throughout the Cell for various functions. ATP contains a purine base, adenine, a sugar, ribose, and three phosphate groups. The adenine and ribose combine to make adenosine. ATP is produced in many ways. Fermentation, respiration and photosynthesis are the main ways. Cells use ADP (Adenosine di-phosphate) and add a phosphate group. After ATP is broken down into ADP and a phosphate group, the parts recycle to form ATP again. The ongoing process of breaking and rejoining phosphate groups is what keeps the cell going
