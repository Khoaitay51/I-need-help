Definition. Myeloid sarcoma is a tissue mass that effaces tissue architecture. It is composed of blasts (ie, granulocytic, monocytic, erythroid, or megakaryocytic precursors) and occurs at anatomic sites outside of the bone marrow. Because myeloid sarcoma represents the tissue mass form of various subtypes of acute myeloid leukemia (AML), the diagnosis is equivalent to a diagnosis of AML.
