Myeloid sarcoma may be composed of the same cell types as AML—that is, granulocytic precursors, monocytic precursors, erythroid precursors, or even megakaryocytic precursors. Various types of myeloid sarcoma are named in accordance with the type of cell that composes the sarcoma (see Disorders, below). Because myeloid sarcoma represents the tissue mass form of various subtypes of acute myeloid leukemia (AML), the diagnosis is equivalent to a diagnosis of AML.
