Reckless Endangerment may be defined as Class A Misdemeanor or as a more serious Felony Offense. If committed with a deadly weapon, Reckless Endangerment becomes a Class E Felony Offense. If the person commits Reckless Endangerment by discharging a firearm into a habitation (a home), the offense is a Class C Felony. In Tennessee, a person may be convicted of the crime of Reckless Endangerment if the state prosecutor proves beyond a reasonable doubt that the person: 1  Recklessly engaged in conduct; 2  That placed or may have placed a person; 3  In imminent danger of death; 4  Or serious bodily injury.
