Lesson Summary. Potassium permanganate, also known as permanganate of potash or Condy's crystals, is a chemical compound with a chemical formula of KMnO4, made of a potassium (K+) ion and a permanganate (MnO4-) ion. It is a strong oxidizing agent, so it readily takes or accepts electrons from other substances. It normally appears in the form of solid purple crystals and can dissolve in water to form a purple solution. Potassium permanganate, while not as well-known as other chemical compounds, has many uses. Organic and Analytical Chemistry. In organic and analytical chemistry laboratories, potassium permanganate is a very valuable reagent. In organic chemistry, potassium permanganate is used to synthesize and react with different organic compounds.
