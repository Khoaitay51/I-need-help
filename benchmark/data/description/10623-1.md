Symptoms. There are five different syndromes associated with lacunar infarcts, and which one a patient experiences depends on where in the brain the arterial blockage and subsequent damage to the brain occurs. The most common syndrome is a pure motor stroke, which affects up to half of stroke patients. A lacunar infarct gets its name from the changes that are grossly visible in the brain when it is examined postmortem. The parts of the brain that have died from lack of oxygen form open spaces, which are called lacunae.
