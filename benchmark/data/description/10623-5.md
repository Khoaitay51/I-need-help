So what is a lacunar infarct? This type of stroke involves blockage of one of the main arteries that supply structures deep inside the brain. Several distinct syndromes, or collections of related symptoms, have been described, which can affect the patient’s ability to recover from the stroke and regain normal function. A lacunar infarct gets its name from the changes that are grossly visible in the brain when it is examined postmortem. The parts of the brain that have died from lack of oxygen form open spaces, which are called lacunae.
