Fair is foul, and foul is fair can be defined as what is good is bad, and what is bad is good. Adding that to the witches' plan later in the play to foo Macbeth into a false sense of security, and you have a play in which truth and good can be easily confused with lies and evil. This line reviles the motives and purpose of the three witches. They are going to turn everything that is fair and pleasant into something foul. What looks pleasant to Macbeth is actually evil and evil is fair to the witches. this contridictory statements suggest the paradox through out the play.
