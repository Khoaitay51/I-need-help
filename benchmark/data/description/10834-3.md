Ringworm affecting the skin is one of several forms of ringworm, which is identified by development of a fungal infection of the topmost layer of skin. It can be identified by a distinct skin rash that is reddish, globular and itchy along with a non-stained, normal skin at the center. 1 It is important to note that patients may become infected by tinea and not experience the distinctive, red rings linked to ringworm rashes. 2  Ringworm does result in itchiness, but generally does not lead to health complications, such as migration of the fungal infection to the deeper layers of the skin.
