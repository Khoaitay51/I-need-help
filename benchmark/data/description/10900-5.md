An amoeba is a type of unicellular organism usually found in water around decaying vegetation, in wet soil and in animals such as humans. It is relatively advanced, and can extend and retract blob-like arms called pseudopodia, which it can use to grasp food particles and to propel itself through microscopic terrain. Amoebas use arms called pseudopods to acquire food. The transparency of most amoebas makes them ideal subjects for microscopic study. It is believed that amoeba were first viewed under a microscope in the mid-1700s. The protists are a group of life forms that are neither plant nor animal.
