Report Abuse. The Dawes Act was put in act on February 8, 1887 regarding the distribution of land to Native Americans in Oklahoma. Named after its sponsor, U.S. Senator Henry L. Dawes of Massachusetts, the act was amended in 1891 and again in 1906 by the Burke Act. The act remained in effect until 1934. The Dawes Act allotted federal land to Indians in Oklahoma in the 1880s (Oklahoma was just a territory then, not a state). Each Indian family got a number of acres. It also made the Indians US citizens. An Indian family could get 160 acres to farm, 80 acres to ranch (cattle) or 40 acres for just a homestead.
