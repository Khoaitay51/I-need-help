A Discovery Health answered. Antibodies (also called immunoglobulins and gammaglobulins) are produced by white blood cells. Antibodies are Y-shaped proteins that respond to a specific antigen (bacteria, virus or toxin). Every antibody has a special section (located at the tips of the Y branches) that is sensitive to a specific antigen and can bind to it. When an antibody binds to a virus particle's outer coat or the cell wall of a bacterium it can stop the virus or bacterium's movement through cell walls. A large number of antibodies can latch on to an invader and signal the complement system that it must remove that invader.
