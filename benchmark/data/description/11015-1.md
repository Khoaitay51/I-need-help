Hail is formed by the layered freezing of water into ice in a strong thunderstorm. Hail is formed when clouds, specifically cumulonimbus clouds, have both high altitudes and powerful updrafts within the condensation layer of the cloud. As rain droplets form, they are frozen and then carried aloft again, where their temperature condenses additional water on the exterior, which then also freezes. When hail is formed, it is not instanteous nor fall immediately. Storms have a peculiarosity of up and down flowing winds.
