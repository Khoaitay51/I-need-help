The three-dimensional shape of a protein is determined by its primary structure. The order of amino acids establishes a protein's structure and specific function. The distinct instructions for the order of amino acids are designated by the genes in a cell. 1 There are two types of secondary structures observed in proteins. 2  One type is the alpha (α) helix structure. 3  This structure resembles a coiled spring and is secured by hydrogen bonding in the polypeptide chain. 4  The second type of secondary structure in proteins is the beta (β) pleated sheet.
