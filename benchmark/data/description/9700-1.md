Not always 1 hour's difference. Some countries have non-standard time zones, usually with a 30-minute offset (a few have a 45-minute offset). For example, the time zone in India is five hours and 30 minutes (UTC+5:30) ahead of UTC. Time zones’ boundaries are irregular mainly because of political factors, and so this has been a subject of criticism. How Time Zones Work. Each time zone is then theoretically 15 degrees wide, corresponding to a one-hour difference in mean solar time. The shape of time zones is changed, in practice, to match internal and international borders.
