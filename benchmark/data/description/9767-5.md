1 Introduction. Jitter is a variation in packet transit delay caused by queuing, contention and serialization effects on the path through the network. In general, higher levels of jitter are more likely to occur on either slow or heavily congested links. (i) Type A – constant jitter. This is a roughly constant level of packet to packet delay variation. (ii) Type B – transient jitter. This is characterized by a substantial incremental delay that may be incurred by a single packet.
