Basic Types of Trusts. The person who creates a trust is called the grantor, the person who manages the trust is called the trustee (who may also be the grantor), and those people or organizations for whom the trust is created are called beneficiaries. A trust may be either revocable or irrevocable. With a revocable trust, the grantor retains full control of the assets placed in the trust, may remove assets from the trust, may change the beneficiaries, and may cancel (or revoke) the trust entirely. With an irrevocable trust, the grantor gives up this type of control
