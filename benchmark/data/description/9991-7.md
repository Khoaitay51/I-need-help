According to OED, baluster is derived through the French: balustre, from Italian: balaustro, from balaustra, pomegranate flower [from a resemblance to the swelling form of the half-open flower (illustration, below left)], from Latin balaustium, from Greek balaustion - βαλαύστιον. Individually, a baluster shaft may describe the turned form taken by a brass or silver candlestick, an upright furniture support, or the stem of a brass chandelier, etc.
