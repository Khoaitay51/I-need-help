The component stems of a compound may be of the same part of speech—as in the case of the English word footpath, composed of the two nouns foot and path —or they may belong to different parts of speech, as in the case of the English word blackbird, composed of the adjective black and the noun bird. Also common in English is another type of verb–noun (or noun–verb) compound, in which an argument of the verb is incorporated into the verb, which is then usually turned into a gerund, such as breastfeeding, finger-pointing, etc. The noun is often an instrumental complement.
