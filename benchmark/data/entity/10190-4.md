When rocks are weathered and eroded (worn down), they break up into smaller pieces called sediment. Over time, the sediment may be moved by wind and rain to rivers, lakes, and oceans. Other sediments form from the shells of animals and from plants. Eventually, these sediments get buried and are squashed together to form sedimentary rocks. Sedimentary rocks make up 80–90 per cent of the rocks on the Earth’s surface. 1  Rock cycle. Fossil of shell. Fossils are very common in sedimentary rocks, such as limestone, but rare in metamorphic rocks, such as marble. Fossils are never found in igneous rocks, such as granite. Sedimentary rock layers settle over the dead animals and plants that then turn into fossils.
