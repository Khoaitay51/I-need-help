Both the raccoon's common name and scientific name refer to its dexterity with its paws. Raccoon is believed to have originated from the Algonquin Indian word aroughcoune which means he scratches with his hands. The Latin species name for raccoons, lotor, translates into washer. Raccoons are extremely common in Connecticut and are gray-brown or silvery, grizzled gray. Their most distinguishing features are the black mask of fur across their eyes that is edged with white and their bushy, 7 1/2 to 16 inch, black-ringed tail.
