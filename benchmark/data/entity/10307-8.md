One aspect of raccoon behavior is so well known that it gives the animal part of its scientific name, Procyon lotor; lotor is neo-Latin for washer. In the wild, raccoons often dabble for underwater food near the shore-line. The word raccoon was adopted into English from the native Powhatan term, as used in the Virginia Colony. It was recorded on Captain John Smith 's list of Powhatan words as aroughcun, and on that of William Strachey as arathkone.
