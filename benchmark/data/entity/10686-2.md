Gerhard Domagk. Gerhard Domagk was born in 1895 in Lagow, Brandenburg, as the son of a teacher. At the age of 20, Domagk — who would later go on to win a Nobel Prize — was confronted with the supposed limits of medicine through his experiences in World War I. At that time, operations that were initially successful were often marred by fatal infectious diseases such as gangrene or gas gangrene. In 1932, while searching for a way to combat bacterial infections by chemical means, Domagk stumbled upon the active substance dimethyl benzyl dodecyl ammonium chloride, which was later launched on the market as a 10 percent solution under the trade name Zephirol.
