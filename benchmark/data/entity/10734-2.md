The fifth strongest metal is an alloy called inconel, which is mostly nickel, but has large amounts of chromium and iron as well, and smaller amounts of niobium, molybdenum, titanium, and aluminum. The tensile strength of a material is the amount of pulling force that can be applied before it weakens and breaks. If something were suspended from a wire, the amount of weight that could be suspended before the wire snapped would be the wire's tensile strength.
