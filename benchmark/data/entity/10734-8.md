The sixth strongest metal is another iron alloy, tool steel, with tungsten, molybdenum, chromium and vanadium added. No. 7 is a tungsten alloy with nickel and copper. Eight and nine are a titanium alloy with aluminum added, and a cobalt-chromium alloy with additions of tungsten, nickel, and carbon. The tensile strength of a material is the amount of pulling force that can be applied before it weakens and breaks. If something were suspended from a wire, the amount of weight that could be suspended before the wire snapped would be the wire's tensile strength.
