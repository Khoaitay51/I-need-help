While other theories of color vision explain how color is processed by the eye, opponent-process theory explains how it is processed by the brain. Once information about color is detected by the retina or membrane in the back of the eye, that information is sent to an area of the brain called the thalamus. Opponent-process theory was first proposed by Ewald Hering in 1874 and states that we perceive color in terms of opposite ends of the spectrum, specifically red to green, yellow to blue, and white to black.
