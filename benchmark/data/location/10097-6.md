Shanty towns are mostly found in developing nations, or partially developed nations with an unequal distribution of wealth (or, on occasion, developed countries in a severe recession). In extreme cases, shanty towns have populations approaching that of a city. Shanty towns tend to develop on the outskirts of cities. During the Great Depression of the 1930s, shanty towns, often called Hobo jungles, favelas, or Georgie Slums appeared in cities across North America because of massive unemployment.
