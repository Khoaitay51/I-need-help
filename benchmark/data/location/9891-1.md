Born to the Lemhi tribe of Shoshone Indians in present-day Idaho in about 1788, Sacagawea would grow up to be a near-legendary figure for her indispensable role on the Lewis and Clark Expedition. The expedition returned to the Hidatsa-Mandan villages in August, 1806, where Charbonneau and Sacagawea parted with Lewis and Clark and spent the next three years living among the among the Hidatsa. For his efforts, Charbonneau was paid $500.33 and 320 acres of land.
