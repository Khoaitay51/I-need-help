If a species sticks around and looks after the eggs until they hatch, and even watches over the baby fish, usually less eggs are laid. For example, Cod lay up to 9 million eggs, but these are left to drift where they will, and so many don't survive even to hatch. Herrings, which lay around 50,000 eggs, also abandon them, but the eggs stick to the bottom and are less likely to be eaten by predators. That is how this fish lays eggs.   Actually, it depends on the fish. Some fish, like salmon, lay only a few hundred eggs. But generally, marine fish lay more eggs. Some marine fish lay millions of eggs.
