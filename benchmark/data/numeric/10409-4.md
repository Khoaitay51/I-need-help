Angiosperms exhibit double fertilization: one sperm unites with an egg, and a second sperm with the central cell of the embryonic sac, or female prothallium. Fertilization independent of the presence of free water is one of the most important adaptations of seed plants to survival on land. The second stage of the reproductive process—karyogamy, or the union of nuclei—occurs within the asci. Somatogamy is a process characteristic of basidiomycetous fungi. These organisms do not form gametes or gametangia, and plasmogamy occurs when two uninuclear cells, the positive and negative primary mycelia, unite.
