Prosecco is more popular than ever in the U.S; according to a Nielsen report, sales rose 32% in 2009. Wine retailers told me much the same thing, though Gerald Weisl of Weimax Liquors in Burlingame, Calif., thought that Champagne producers were partly to blame. I almost never get a chance to taste Prosecco, he said. By the time Mr. Sohm returned, I'd amassed almost 20 bottles of Prosecco ranging from sweet to dry. They included well-known names like Zardetto and Mionetto as well as more obscure labels like Drusian and Bisol. Most cost between $10 and $15.
